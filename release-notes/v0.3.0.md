<!-- title: v0.3.0 - The Native Update -->

The extra session is gone, and with it the Node runtime, the stored token and the background sidecar. Steam Auto Status is now one small native executable that asks the Steam client you already have open to change its status.

## 🪶 One app, no session

Everything the app does happens inside a single small process, using the same local commands as Steam's own Friends > Status menu.

- **No session of its own**: the app never signs in and stores no token. It asks the client that is already open to switch between Online and Invisible. Steam must be open and signed in: if it is closed you are already Offline, and the app applies the schedule as soon as the client starts.
- **Ten times lighter**: the installer drops from 24.5 MB to about 2.5 MB, and the app idles around 35 MB of RAM (it was 140-160 MB with the sidecar). The bundled Node runtime and the background process are gone.
- **Your client stays yours**: it only flips between Online and Invisible (never Offline, which would drop you off friends and chat), never launches games, never signs your client out and never touches your credentials.

## ✨ What's new

- **Panel without sign-in**: the header shows the client state at a glance (open, starting, closed). When the client is closed, the panel warns you and offers to open Steam; the dashboard shows the last status sent with its time. Reapply now sends it again, and since the local command has no reply, the panel shows what was sent, not a live reading of your presence.
- **Upgrading from 0.2.x**: your config and logs are kept, but the old version may have left login files behind. Settings > Steam client detects them and deletes them with one confirmation. Deleting the files does not revoke the old token: revoke it too in Steam (Manage Steam devices, linked from the panel).
- **One less quirk**: a new session used to make Steam's client reload its friends list, sometimes leaving it blank. With no extra sessions, that should not happen anymore.
- **README rebuilt**: the page now explains the new model, what happens when Steam is closed and how to clean up after the old version.
